Here's what you need, honey. One cup of butter, two sticks, salted is fine. One cup of white sugar. Three tablespoons of water. A teaspoon of vanilla. And for the top, one cup of chocolate chips, or a good chocolate bar, chopped, and three quarters of a cup of almonds or pecans, toasted and chopped fine. That's it.

And mistake number one. A thin pot. A thin saucepan heats unevenly, and the sugar burns on the bottom in one spot while the rest isn't ready. Use a heavy saucepan, at least two quarts, with a thick bottom. And a light-colored one if you have it, so you can see the color of the toffee as it cooks.

Get everything ready before you start, sweetheart. Line a baking sheet with parchment or a silicone mat. Sprinkle half the chopped nuts on it. Have the chocolate chopped, the vanilla measured. Because when the toffee is ready, it's ready right then, and you don't have one second to go looking for anything.

Step one. Put the butter, sugar, and water in the pot over medium heat. Stir it gently with a wooden spoon until the butter melts and the sugar dissolves completely. Then clip on your candy thermometer. Mistake number two is turning the heat up too high to rush it. That's the most common reason toffee separates. Keep it at medium, honey.

When butter and sugar get too hot too fast, the butter fat breaks away from the sugar, and you get a greasy layer of melted butter floating on top of a hard sugar lump. That's separating. Steady, medium heat keeps them together, like a good marriage. Mrs. Carrington said, toffee is like a husband. Rush it, and it falls apart.

Mistake number three is grainy toffee. That happens when sugar crystals form on the sides of the pot and fall back in. If you see sugar crystals sticking to the sides above the toffee, brush them down with a wet pastry brush. And don't scrape the sides of the pot at the very end, when you pour it out. And test your thermometer first, honey, in boiling water. It should read two hundred twelve. If it's off by a few degrees, adjust for it. And if you don't have a thermometer, use the old test. Now, right away, while the toffee is still hot, sprinkle the chopped chocolate all over the top. Wait two or three minutes, and the heat from the toffee melts it. Then sprinkle the rest of the chopped nuts over the warm chocolate, and press them in lightly with your hand. And if it does separate, honey, don't throw it out. Take it off the heat, add two tablespoons of very hot water, and stir it hard and fast until it comes back together. Then put it back on the heat, lower this time, and keep cooking. It works most of the time. Mrs. Carrington taught me that, and it's saved my Christmas candy at least four times.

And cleaning the pot, sweetheart. Don't scrub it. Hardened toffee in a pot is like cement. Just fill the pot with water, put it back on the stove, and bring it to a boil.
